# Cognitive walkthrough

## Question 1: Scenario-based task

**Task: Log last night's sleep and make sure the provider gets the right picture**

> You are Maya, a 21-year-old student. Last night you went to bed around 12:30, it took you about an hour to fall asleep, you woke up twice, and you got up at 8:15. You don't remember how long you were awake in the night. You had an energy drink at 4 pm, took a melatonin before bed, and had a bad headache. Log your night, and make sure your primary care provider will know about the melatonin and the headache.

**Why this task:**

- **It's the core of our solution:** a one-minute morning check-in where the patient talks naturally and an AI turns the story into a standard sleep diary.
- **It tests the unique parts:** voice input, AI-filled answers, correcting the AI, "I don't know", recording a sleep medicine, notes for the provider, and the watch-vs-diary question. Login and profiles are standard and not interesting.
- **Medicines matter most to the provider:** they need to know which sleep aid was taken and whether it matches the plan. If a patient can't record this easily, the Snapshot is misleading.
- **It's where users are most likely to struggle:** correcting an AI, admitting "I don't know", and deciding what a provider should see are new to people.

## Question 2: Action sequence (what we expect the participant to do)

| # | Expected action | What the participant should notice or understand |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | On the start screen, tap **Skip to Maya's morning** (the morning reminder pops up; tapping it also works) | Understands they are now Maya |
| 2 | On "How did you sleep?", tap the **microphone** (or **Type it** / **Tap answers**) | Knows they can talk or type |
| 3 | Describe the night in their own words (the six hint tiles show what to cover), then tap **finish** (stop button or Continue) | Talks naturally; no form to fill |
| 4 | On **"Here's what I heard"**, read the answers the AI filled in | Understands the AI wrote these answers |
| 5 | Optionally tap the **✓ circle** next to an answer to confirm it, or tap the line to change it | Confirms with one tap instead of re-entering |
| 6 | In the highlighted **"Awake during the night"** line, pick a rough amount, or leave it as **Don't know** | Knows they don't have to guess |
| 7 | Under **"How did you sleep?"**, tap a face, then tap **Next** | Sees that quality is the only required answer |
| 8 | Optional: tap **"What did the AI do?"** | Learns what the AI does and doesn't do |
| 9 | On **"Anything else?"**, in **"What woke you?"**, pick one or more reasons (optional) | Understands everything on this page is optional |
| 10 | In **Medication**, check that **Melatonin** is selected (or select it) | Knows where to record a sleep medicine |
| 11 | In **"Anything different yesterday?"**, check that **Late caffeine** is selected | Sees the habit was picked up from the story |
| 12 | In **"For your provider"**, check that the headache is listed, or **add it** (type or mic) | Knows this goes to the provider before the visit |
| 13 | Tap **Save** | Done in two short pages |
| 14 | On the Saved screen, answer **"Your watch saw it differently"** if it appears | Understands their own answer counts, not the watch |
| 15 | Look at the **streak and badges**, the **"Last night"** timeline and **"This week"** chart, then tap **Edit** and come back | Reads the results from the pictures, without reading text; knows they can still change the entry |
| 16 | Tap **Back to home** | Done in about 1 minute |

## Question 4: Questions for evaluators after the task

1. What was the hardest or most confusing part?
2. Was anything missing that you wanted to tell your provider?
3. Did you trust what the AI filled in? Why or why not?
4. Was it easy to change an answer that was wrong?
5. Would you do this every morning? What would stop you?
6. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
